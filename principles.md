# Principles

Seven ideas sit underneath everything in this repo. Each one exists because I learned it the expensive way.

## 1. Decisions are the unit of value

Features are what we ship. Decisions are what we are actually good or bad at. When I look at a product org that is struggling, I rarely find a talent problem. I find decisions that should take a day taking a month, because nobody owns them, or everyone does.

So I measure decisions, not just output. How long does a decision of this type take here? Who made it? Did we make it again three months later because nobody wrote it down? (See [`decision-velocity/`](decision-velocity/).)

This matters even more with AI. "Hours saved" is the wrong way to measure AI. "Decisions improved" is the right one.

## 2. Count your roadmaps

Ask each leader to show you the roadmap they work from. If you get more than one answer, you have more than one strategy, and the teams are quietly negotiating between them every day.

One roadmap. One owner. Everything else is a view of it.

## 3. One source of truth

The same principle for data and definitions. If two teams report different numbers for the same metric, the meeting becomes about the numbers instead of the decision. Fix the definition once, publish it, and point every dashboard at it.

## 4. Subtract before you add

Every new initiative has to name what it replaces or stops. Not "deprioritizes." Stops. Capacity is fixed; pretending otherwise just spreads the team thinner until everything ships late.

## 5. Build the plumbing before it is fashionable

The unglamorous platform work (search, data, identity, the internal tools nobody demos) is what lets you move fast later. It never wins the prioritization vote on its own, so I protect a fixed share of capacity for it and do not relitigate it every quarter.

## 6. Growth needs a structure that can carry it

The test I use: is profit growing faster than revenue? If revenue grows and profit does not follow, the operating model is absorbing the growth instead of compounding it. Fix the structure first.

## 7. Agents prepare, people decide

Agents are now part of how a product org works, and they need the same management discipline as people: a role, a named owner, clear decision rights, and a regular review. They are excellent at the slow part of a decision. The decision itself stays with a person who answers for it.

---

*A note on the roadmap itself.* Earlier in my career I shipped work I was proud of into an operating model that did not hold still while we built. If I did it again, I would rebuild the decision cycle that decided what to ship before I shipped anything. That is why this repo starts with decisions.
